# Model paragraphs for the shelf study write up

**SIMULATED DATA.** The three paragraphs below are models for students. Every statistic in them comes from `SIMULATED_shelf_study_AOI_metrics.csv` and `SIMULATED_shelf_study_survey.csv`, which `make_simulated_data.py` generated (seed 20261037) to mirror the iMotions In-Store Shopper Report (Sensodyne POSM versus no POSM) in direction and rough size. No real shopper was recorded, and the numbers must never be quoted as findings. The paragraphs show the form: what belongs in a methods paragraph for the analysis, how to report two group results in APA style (M, SD, t, df, p, d with its confidence interval), and how to write a discussion that names the limitations of a glasses study. Statistics were computed with `analysis.R` (R 4.3.3, lme4 1.1-35, effectsize 0.8.6); `analysis_output.txt` holds the full output.

---

## (a) Methods: data analysis

Gaze from each glasses recording was mapped onto a reference photograph of the shelf, and six areas of interest (AOIs) were drawn on that image: Sensodyne_Products, Sensodyne_POSM_Wing (POSM shelf only), Competitor_A, Competitor_B, Price_Tags, and Shelf_Other. Fixation based AOI metrics were exported per respondent from iMotions Lab 11.1 (Individual AOI metrics export, dwell classification threshold 100 ms, no first fixations excluded). The primary dependent variable was time to first fixation on Sensodyne_Products (TTFF parent, measured from the start of the shelf interval); the secondary dependent variable was the share of the shelf interval spent fixating that AOI (Dwell time (%)). Before inspecting the data we fixed two rules: respondents with Valid data below 70 percent were excluded, and an AOI that a respondent never fixated was treated as TTFF not observed and dwell time zero. One respondent (P17, POSM) was excluded under the first rule because the glasses slipped during the session (Valid data 46 percent), leaving 14 respondents on the POSM shelf and 15 on the NoPOSM shelf. Each hypothesis was tested with Welch's t test for independent groups, and the effect size was expressed as Cohen's d with pooled standard deviation and a 95 percent confidence interval from the noncentral t distribution. Because TTFF is right skewed and the groups are small, each t test was repeated as a Wilcoxon rank sum test. To examine attention across all AOIs at once without treating a respondent's AOI rows as independent, dwell share was modeled with a linear mixed effects model with AOI, condition, and their interaction as fixed effects and a random intercept for respondent (lme4), with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom (lmerTest); the POSM wing was omitted from that model because it exists on one shelf only. Survey responses were compared with Welch's t test (purchase intent) and Fisher's exact test (brand recall, product chosen). All tests were two tailed with alpha = .05, and all numbers reported here are simulated for teaching.

## (b) Results

Shoppers who saw the POSM shelf fixated Sensodyne products sooner than shoppers who saw the shelf without POSM. Time to first fixation on Sensodyne_Products was shorter in the POSM condition (M = 2.25 s, SD = 0.86, n = 14) than in the NoPOSM condition (M = 3.59 s, SD = 0.98, n = 15), a mean difference of 1.34 s, 95% CI [0.64, 2.04], Welch t(26.89) = 3.91, p < .001, d = 1.45, 95% CI [0.61, 2.26]. A Wilcoxon rank sum test agreed (W = 184, p < .001). Dwell time share on Sensodyne_Products was also higher with POSM (M = 18.43%, SD = 5.98) than without (M = 10.76%, SD = 4.42), a difference of 7.68 percentage points, 95% CI [3.62, 11.73], t(23.87) = 3.91, p < .001, d = 1.47, 95% CI [0.63, 2.28]; Wilcoxon W = 27, p < .001. Every kept respondent fixated Sensodyne_Products at least once, so the respondent ratio was 100 percent in both conditions and TTFF max. equaled TTFF parent. Time on the two competitor AOIs combined was 27 percent lower with POSM (M = 35.99 s, SD = 14.56) than without (M = 49.33 s, SD = 16.36), t(26.95) = 2.32, p = .028, d = 0.86, 95% CI [0.09, 1.62]. In the mixed model of dwell share across the five shared AOIs, the AOI by condition interaction was significant, F(4, 108) = 8.14, p < .001, reflecting the shift of attention toward Sensodyne_Products and away from Competitor_B with POSM; the random intercept for respondent (SD = 3.20 percentage points, residual SD = 4.79) indicated that about 31 percent of the variance in dwell share lay between shoppers. Stated purchase intent for Sensodyne was higher after the POSM shelf (M = 4.86, SD = 1.03) than after the NoPOSM shelf (M = 4.13, SD = 1.36), but the difference did not reach significance, t(25.95) = 1.63, p = .116, d = 0.60, 95% CI [-0.15, 1.34]. Brand recall (12 of 14 versus 8 of 15, Fisher's exact p = .109) and choice of a Sensodyne product (7 of 14 versus 3 of 15, p = .120) followed the same direction. Across conditions, dwell share on Sensodyne_Products correlated with purchase intent, r = .49, 95% CI [.15, .73], p = .007. (All values simulated.)

## (c) Discussion

In these simulated data, point of sale material changed where shoppers looked first and how they divided their attention across the shelf: Sensodyne products were fixated about 1.3 seconds sooner, received a larger share of looking time, and drew attention away from competitor packages, which is the pattern the iMotions In-Store Shopper Report described for its Copenhagen sample. The self report measures moved in the same direction but with smaller and less certain effects, a reminder that attention and stated intent are related but not interchangeable and that fifteen respondents per shelf give little power for survey outcomes. Several limitations are typical of mobile eye tracking in a store and would apply to a real version of this study. First, every AOI metric depends on gaze mapping: only the portion of each recording that the algorithm mapped onto the reference photograph contributes, so lighting changes, blurred frames, and moments when the shopper turned away reduce coverage, and the Valid data metric and visual checks of the mapped replay should be reported alongside the results; the one exclusion here shows how a slipped frame can invalidate a session. Second, fixation classification on glasses data mixes head and eye movement, and iMotions notes that gaze based metrics can be preferred for mobile recordings, so the fixation based results should be checked against gaze based dwell and hit time. Third, the sample is small and the design is between subjects, so the large effect sizes carry wide confidence intervals (for TTFF, d between roughly 0.6 and 2.3), and a replication with more shoppers per shelf is needed before any practical recommendation. Fourth, the data come from one store, one shelf, and one brand, and the POSM wing also changed the shelf's physical layout, so the effect cannot be separated from the extra space and color the wing added. Fifth, wearing glasses and being asked to shop a specific category creates demand effects: shoppers know they are observed and may inspect the shelf more thoroughly than they would unobserved, which inflates dwell times and may compress differences between conditions. Finally, these numbers are simulated for teaching and were generated to resemble the published pattern; they demonstrate the analysis, not the effect.
