# Study planning checklist: from the question to the pilot

**PLANNED STUDY, SIMULATED PLANNING DATA.** This checklist is a teaching tool for Workshop 9. The worked example column applies it to the PLANNED follow up ad study (fictional brands ALTAVO and BRENLOW; codes only; placeholder dates). Nothing here describes a real study, client, or participant. Lab rules are quoted from the lab procedure manual; iMotions steps are quoted from the iMotions manuals in `ws9/manuals_src`; anything not found there is marked as an item to verify.

**How to use it.** Work down the list in order. An item is done only when the "Done looks like" column is true and the evidence is filed in the study folder (Project doc 06, a proposal; lab procedure manual section 8.2). Items 1 to 9 can be drafted before the IRB submission; items 10 to 12 must be complete before it; items 13 to 18 come after approval. No participant is contacted before item 12 is done (lab procedure manual, section 2.1).

## The checklist

| 1 | **Question** | One sentence that names the decision the result will inform, written with the client, and the smallest difference that would change that decision. | iMotions, Designing Your Research Study ("identify the specific business question ... avoid taking the objective from a research brief at face value"); Project doc 01 | "Is ALTAVO advertising liked more than BRENLOW advertising, beyond the particular ads?" Smallest difference that matters: 0.50 points on the 1 to 7 liking scale. |
| 2 | **Comparison** | The conditions are named, and for each hypothesis the comparison and the outcome are written down: what is compared with what, in which measure. | Designing Your Research Study (hypotheses before data collection; avoid a "fishing expedition"); Workshop 8 `results_text.md`, "What the next study needs" | ALTAVO ads versus BRENLOW ads on liking (primary); purchase intention, Joy, and GSR peaks per minute (secondary family); Logo fixation and recall, Joy and liking, GSR versus Baseline (replications of Workshop 7). |
| 3 | **Design** | Between or within subjects is decided with a reason; the number of conditions and stimuli is fixed; the session fits the time participants can attend without fatigue. | Designing Your Research Study (between and within subjects; "How much content can I expose someone to?"; mixed designs); Project doc 01 | Within subjects: every participant sees every ad. Ads are a random factor so that a brand claim can generalize to other ads of the brands (Judd et al., 2012). |
| 4 | **Stimuli** | Every stimulus file exists in its final form, with the same length, resolution, and layout; the AOIs are defined on each; the file names follow the plan. | Lab procedure manual, section 8.1 (naming); Workshop 2 (AOIs); Using a Test Plan for Study Design (a "Stimuli" folder next to the test plan file) | 12 video ads of 30 s: 6 per brand, one campaign per brand, the same layout so that the Logo, Product, and Headline AOIs sit in the same place in every ad. | 5 | **Sensors and licenses** | Each measure is tied to a sensor, the sensor to a license module, and the module to the computer that will record. | 11 | **Consent** | The consent form is written on the CURRENT JSU IRB template, says what every sensor records, says that face video identifies the person, states storage, access, and retention, and asks for any optional use of face video separately with a written release. | `consent_form_model.md` (a teaching model only); lab procedure manual, sections 2.2 and 3.5; iMotions, Respondent informed consent and data privacy FAQ; Project doc 01, section 7 | Adults only; about 60 minutes; optional permissions A to D; placeholders for everything the IRB decides. |
| 12 | **IRB submission** | The application is submitted and APPROVED (or exempted) before any participant is contacted; the approval letter and the approved consent form are filed; the IRB number is written in the lab logbook; everyone who collects data has current CITI training. | Lab procedure manual, section 2.1 (approval before scheduling; documentation on file; CITI training; any change needs an amendment) | Items to verify with the JSU IRB: forms, review level, timelines, the consent template, rules for course credit. |
| 13 | **Recruitment** | The approved recruitment text and channel are used exactly; eligibility and screening questions are those the IRB approved; recruitment stops at the stopping rule. | Approved IRB protocol; Designing Your Research Study ("Sampling and Random Assignment") | Recruit until 48 participants meet the rules (cap 60 sessions); the approved text says about 60 minutes and [compensation]. |
| 14 | **Scheduling and staffing** | Every slot is in the lab calendar with a participant code and two staff codes; setup, cleaning, and backup time are inside the slots; reserve and open slots exist; staff have done the lab orientation. | `SIMULATED_session_schedule.csv`; lab procedure manual, sections 3.1 (sign in, reservation calendar), 7.1 (session workflow), 7.2 (stagger at least 20 to 30 min; adequate staffing); iMotions, Staffing ("Running a pilot test is critical ... to determine how many data collectors are needed") | 90 min slots, 5 per weekday, 11 weekdays, pairs S1 and S2, S3 and S4, S5 and S6 by half day; setup from 08:15. |
| 15 | **Session script** | A word for word script from greeting to debriefing, with the consent step, the sensor explanation in lay terms, the calibration instruction, and what to do if something fails; printed at the station. | Lab procedure manual, section 7.1.3 to 7.1.6; iMotions, Sample Data Collection Scripts ("Video Ad" script) | Adapt the iMotions Video Ad script (GSR, ET, FEA) to the lab's Shimmer and Aurora; add the brand recall instructions. |
| 16 | **Cleaning** | A between participant routine with times is part of every slot; supplies are stocked; the routine is the manual's. | Lab procedure manual, section 3.4 and Appendix E; iMotions, How to properly sanitize your data collection site between participants | Wipe the finger electrodes and straps with alcohol, wipe surfaces, leave the room empty at least 10 minutes; 30 minutes after each participant. |
| 17 | **Pilot** | At least two people run end to end with the real stimuli, script, and timing; the data are exported; the primary measures come out of the export as planned; problems and timings are logged. | 18 | **Go or no go** | A short written decision against criteria set in advance; any change to the protocol goes to the IRB as an amendment BEFORE data collection; the analysis plan is dated and frozen. | Lab procedure manual, section 2.1 (amendments); `analysis_plan_prereg.md` | Go if: both pilots finish within 60 minutes; the Aurora calibrates; eye tracking quality is at least 90 percent and face quality at least 80 percent on the pilot ads; GSR shows peaks; all exports open in the planned pipeline. Otherwise fix, amend if needed, and pilot again. |

## During data collection (after "go")

- **Monitoring rule.** After every 12 sessions (one block), count the exclusions. If more than 3 of a block's participants fail rule R2, pause and look for a procedural cause (calibration, lighting, seating) before continuing; that is a protocol problem, not bad luck (the Workshop 7 rate was 2 of 24).
- **No peeking.** Quality checks are allowed after each session; the outcome variables are not analyzed before the stopping rule is met (`analysis_plan_prereg.md`, section 3.5).
- **Session log.** One row per recording, as in Workshop 7: participant code, recording number, date, start time, seat, computer, operator, order (Latin square row), Shimmer unit, Aurora calibration, use recording, notes.
- **Changes.** Any change, "even seemingly minor ones (such as adding a questionnaire)", needs an IRB amendment (lab procedure manual, section 2.1).

## Items to verify

1. The JSU IRB forms, review levels, required training, consent template, and timelines (only from JSU's own pages or the IRB office; not taken from any JSU page for this checklist).
2. Whether the lab adopts the folder plan of Project doc 06 and the session log columns of Workshop 7 (both proposals).
3. How the Williams order is entered in iMotions Lab 11.1: one study flow per row (Using the iMotions Study Builder) or a test plan CSV (Using a Test Plan for Study Design). The test plan article says that respondents cannot be added after recording starts, so reserve codes must be in the file from the start; the article's examples come from older versions and must be tried on the lab computer.
4. The sensor setup time without the EEG cap (planned 10 minutes; the manual's 20 to 30 minutes include the EEG), measured in the pilot.
5. The lab's academic calendar closures for the planned weeks.
