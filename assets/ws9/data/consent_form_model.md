# TEACHING MODEL: consent form for the planned follow up ad study

> **TEACHING MODEL FOR CLASS USE ONLY. NOT APPROVED. DO NOT GIVE THIS FORM TO ANY PARTICIPANT.**
> This is a PLANNED study with SIMULATED planning data (Workshop 9). The form shows students what a consent form for a biometric ad study must cover and how to say it in plain language. Before any real study, this text must be replaced by the CURRENT Jacksonville State University (JSU) IRB consent template, completed for the actual study, and approved by the JSU IRB. The lab procedure manual (section 2.1) requires IRB approval or exemption before any participant is scheduled and an approved consent form on file. Everything in [square brackets] is decided by the JSU IRB, the lab, or the study team; nothing in brackets may be guessed. ALTAVO and BRENLOW are invented brands. No real person, client, or school appears here.

**How to read this file.** Part 1 is the model form, written for an eighth grade reader. Part 2 is a model assent paragraph for a minor. Part 3 is a note on parental permission. Part 4 lists, for teaching, where each element comes from. Parts 2 to 4 are not part of the form.

## Part 1. The model form

**Consent to Take Part in a Research Study**

**Study title:** [Title approved by the IRB] (working title: How People React to Water Bottle Ads)
**Principal investigator:** [Name, title, department]
**Contact:** [Email] [Phone]
**IRB approval number:** [Number] **Approval date:** [Date] [Expiration date, if the template has one]

### Key facts about this study

- We are asking you to take part in a research study. It is your choice.
- You will watch 12 short video ads for water bottles on a computer. You will answer a few questions about each ad.
- It takes about 60 minutes. You come to the lab one time.
- Three sensors will record how you react. A camera will record video of your face.
- Your face video can show who you are. We will protect it. Section 7 tells you how.
- The risks are small. The finger sensors may make your skin a little red for a short time.
- You will get [compensation].
- You can stop at any time. You do not have to give a reason.

Please read the rest of this form. Ask us about anything that is not clear.

### 1. Why are we doing this study?

We want to learn how people pay attention to ads and how they feel about them. We will compare ads for two brands of water bottles. The brands are made up for this study. The results will help us learn what makes an ad work. [If the study is done for a client: The study is done for [type of client, as approved by the IRB]. The client will see only group results. The client will never see your name or your face.]

### 2. What will I do?

If you agree, you will do these things in one visit to the lab in [building and room]:

1. **Get ready (about 25 minutes).** We go over this form with you. We ask a few health questions (see section 4). We put two small sensors on two fingers. We check that the eye tracker can see your eyes. You will follow a dot on the screen with your eyes.
2. **Watch and answer (about 20 minutes).** You will look at a gray screen for one minute. Then you will watch 12 ads. Each ad is 30 seconds long. After each ad, you will answer two questions about it. At the end, you will answer some questions about the ads and about yourself, such as your age.
3. **Finish (about 15 minutes).** We take the sensors off. We tell you more about the study and answer your questions. [We give you your compensation.]

Please sit still while the ads play. You can blink and breathe as usual. You can ask for a break at any time. A researcher will stay close by the whole time. You can talk to them at any time.

### 3. What do the sensors record?

| Sensor | What it is | What it records | Can it show who you are? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eye tracker | A thin bar below the screen. It shines a light you cannot see (infrared) toward your eyes. A small camera uses that light to find your eyes. | Where you look on the screen, and how your eyes move. | No. It records numbers about your eyes, not a picture of your face. [Lab to confirm on the lab computer whether any eye image is stored.] |
| Webcam | A small camera on top of the screen. | A video of your face while you watch the ads. A computer program measures face movements, such as a smile or a frown. | **Yes.** The video shows your face. |
| Skin sensor | A small box worn on your hand or wrist. Two small sensors rest on two fingers. Soft straps hold them in place. | Tiny changes in sweat on your skin. These changes show how strongly your body reacts. You will not feel anything from the sensor. [If the pulse sensor is used: a small clip on [your earlobe or a finger, as the lab decides] records your heart rate.] | No. It records numbers, not pictures. |
| Questions | Questions on the screen. | Your answers about the ads, your age, and your gender. | Not by themselves. |

The computer also records what was on the screen and when. It does not record sound. [Lab to confirm that no microphone is recording.]

### 4. Can I take part?

You can take part if you are [18 years of age or older] and can see a computer screen well. You may wear glasses or contacts. Before we start, we will ask you some questions to keep you safe. Please tell us if you:

- have a skin allergy, for example to sensor gel, tape, or alcohol wipes;
- have ever had a seizure, or if flashing lights bother you;
- have a cut, rash, or sore on your fingers.

You may not be able to take part if one of these applies to you. That is for your safety. [The IRB approved list of reasons a person cannot take part.]

**Before your visit, please:** wash your hands and do not use lotion that day; do not wear eye makeup; take off rings before the session if you can. [Other instructions approved by the IRB.]

### 5. What are the risks?

The risks are small. They are about the same as using a computer for an hour.

- **Skin:** The finger sensors may leave your skin a little red. Strap marks fade soon. If we use a small amount of sensor gel, it may feel cool or sticky. We wipe it off at the end.
- **Eyes and tiredness:** Looking at a screen may make your eyes or body tired. You can ask for a break at any time.
- **Flashing images:** The ads do not contain flashing lights. If flashing lights bother you, please tell us before we start.
- **Feelings:** An ad may not be to your liking. You can look away or stop at any time.
- **Privacy:** Your face video and your answers are kept private. Still, there is a small chance that someone who should not see them could see them. Section 7 tells you how we protect your data.

If you feel any discomfort, tell the researcher. We will stop right away.

### 6. Will I benefit?

You will not benefit directly. You may learn how ad research works. Your help may teach us how people react to ads.

### 7. How will you protect my information?

- **A code, not your name.** Your data will have a code, such as P07. Your name will not be on any data file.
- **Your signed form is kept apart from your data.** We keep this signed form in [a locked cabinet in a locked room]. We do not store it with the data.
- **Safe storage.** The data are saved on a computer that needs a password. [The data are stored on encrypted drives approved by the IRB.]
- **Where the data are processed.** The study software (iMotions) stores the data on the lab computer. [If any online or cloud service is used: name of the service, where the data are stored and processed, and how long they are kept. The IRB must approve this.]
- **Who sees your data.** Only the researchers named on the approved study plan can see your face video and your data. [List any others the IRB approves.]
- **What we share.** We report results only as group averages, such as "most viewers looked at the bottle." We will never put your name in a report. We will not show your face to anyone outside the research team unless you give us written permission in section 10.
- [Any limits to privacy that the IRB template requires us to tell you.]

### 8. How long will you keep my data?

We will keep your data for [the number of years the IRB approves]. Then we will delete it in a safe way. We will shred the paper forms. [If the IRB allows it: data without your face and without any link to you may be kept longer for research.]

### 9. Do I have to take part? Can I stop?

No. Taking part is your choice. You can say no. You can stop at any time, even in the middle of the session. You do not have to give a reason. Nothing bad will happen if you say no or stop. [If course credit is offered: Saying no will not affect your grade. You can earn the same credit another way: [the other option approved by the IRB].]

If you stop, you can ask us to delete your data. We can do this until [the date or step the IRB approves, for example until the data no longer carry a link to you].

### 10. Optional permissions

These are extra. You can take part in the study even if you say no to all of them. Please mark one box for each and write your initials.

| | Yes | No | Initials |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. You may show short clips or still pictures of my face in classes at JSU to teach how this research works. **I will also sign the separate written release form.** | [ ] | [ ] | ____ |
| B. You may show short clips or still pictures of my face in talks or papers about this study. **I will also sign the separate written release form.** | [ ] | [ ] | ____ |
| C. You may keep my data without my face and without my name for future research. [Only if the IRB allows this.] | [ ] | [ ] | ____ |
| D. You may contact me about future studies. My email: ______________ | [ ] | [ ] | ____ |

If you say yes to A or B, you can change your mind later. Tell us in writing, and we will stop using your clips from then on.

### 11. Who can I contact?

- **Questions about the study:** [Name of the principal investigator], [email], [phone].
- **Questions about your rights, or a concern you want to raise with someone outside the research team:** [the JSU IRB contact, exactly as given on the current JSU IRB template].

You will get a copy of this form to keep.

### 12. Your consent

I have read this form, or someone read it to me. I had the chance to ask questions. My questions were answered. I agree to take part in this study. I know that I can stop at any time.

Name (print): ______________________________

Signature: ______________________________ Date: ____________

Researcher (print): ______________________________

Researcher signature: ______________________________ Date: ____________

## Part 2. Model assent paragraph for a minor (teaching model)

> Use only if the IRB approves a study with minors. The planned follow up study enrolls adults only. An assent form must be approved by the IRB in the same way as a consent form, and a parent or guardian must also give permission (Part 3).

**Will you help us with a study?** We want to learn how people look at ads. If you say yes, you will watch some short ads on a computer and answer questions about them. A camera will record your face while you watch. Two small sensors will sit on two of your fingers. They do not hurt. It will take about [number] minutes. You can stop at any time, and nobody will be upset with you. Your name will not be on your answers. Your parent or guardian said it is okay for you to be asked, but it is still your choice. You can ask us questions at any time. If you want to take part, please write your name below.

Your name: ______________________ Date: __________ Researcher: ______________________

## Part 3. Note on parental permission (not part of the form)

1. **Who is a minor** depends on state law and the IRB's rules. Do not assume the age; the JSU IRB states the age at which a person can consent for themselves (item to verify with the JSU IRB).
2. **Two documents.** Research with minors normally needs the permission of a parent or guardian AND the assent (agreement) of the minor. Federal rules for research with children are in 45 CFR 46, Subpart D. The IRB decides whether one or two parents must sign, whether assent must be written, and which wording to use.
3. **The lab's own rule.** The lab procedure manual (section 7.1.3) notes that if a participant is a minor, a guardian would be present to consent.
4. **Codes of practice.** The NMSBA Code of Ethics has an article on research with children (Article 8; Project doc 01 lists the code). The iMotions respondent consent FAQ reminds the researcher that the rules depend on the country and that the researcher, not iMotions, is responsible for following them.
5. **From the lab's own experience.** The lab has run one remote study with minors for a campus client. Remote studies with minors add steps: the parent's permission and the minor's assent must both be collected before the study link opens, the face video is recorded at home, and the consent text must say where the video is stored and processed (the iMotions FAQ asks for this whenever a cloud product is used).
6. **The planned follow up study enrolls adults only.** If a client later asks to include minors, the study needs a new IRB submission or an amendment before any minor is contacted (lab procedure manual, section 2.1).
