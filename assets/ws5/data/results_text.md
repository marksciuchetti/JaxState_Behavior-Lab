# Model paragraphs for the EEG frontal alpha asymmetry write up

**SIMULATED DATA.** The paragraphs below are models for students. Every statistic in them comes from the files `SIMULATED_eeg_fa_metrics.csv`, `SIMULATED_eeg_fa_metrics_default_channels.csv`, `SIMULATED_eeg_psd_metrics.csv`, `SIMULATED_eeg_artifact_report.csv`, `SIMULATED_eeg_session_log.csv`, and `SIMULATED_eeg_survey.csv`, which `make_simulated_data.py` generated (seed 20267088) in the style of the iMotions demo study "DEMO - SonyBravia". No real person was recorded, and the numbers must never be quoted as findings. Nothing about the content of the Sony commercials was used. The paragraphs show the form: what belongs in a methods paragraph for a frontal alpha asymmetry (FA) study with the Enobio 8, how to report a within subjects test, a between subjects test with a power note, and a mixed model in APA style (M, SD, t, df, p, and an effect size with its confidence interval), how to report sensitivity analyses, and how to write a discussion that names the limits of an FA study. Statistics were computed with `analysis.R` (R 4.3.3, lme4 1.1.35.1, lmerTest 3.1.3; effect sizes and their noncentral t intervals were computed by hand and cross-checked with the effectsize package); `analysis_output.txt` holds the full output, and every number below is copied from it (p values are rounded to three decimals). FA is in natural log units. Every change score is a segment minus the FA of the baseline that immediately preceded its commercial.

**Parts of the pipeline are assumed or modeled and must be verified on the lab computer before real data are analyzed.** First, the channel map is assumed from the lab manual montage order (Ch5 = F3 and Ch6 = F4 were entered as the left and right frontal electrode sets); the true map is set by the NIC2 protocol. Second, the artifact report table, the session log, and the survey are modeled layouts. Third, the simulation computes a stimulus mean FA as the mean of the 1 s window FA values; the notebook article does not say whether it averages the window FA values or the powers.

## (a) Methods: data analysis

Forty-eight respondents were randomly assigned, in blocks of four, to one of two versions of Commercial 1 and to one of two viewing orders (12 per cell). Version A saw Commercial_1A (60 s), which contains a 6 s key scene at 32 to 38 s; Version B saw Commercial_1B (54 s), the same commercial with the key scene cut. All respondents also saw Commercial_2 (60 s). Each session consisted of Baseline_1 (a 60 s neutral nature video), the first commercial, a rating slide, Baseline_2 (a second 60 s nature video), the second commercial, and a second rating slide; within each version, half of the respondents saw Commercial 1 first. EEG was recorded with a Neuroelectrics Enobio 8 at 500 Hz (USB connection, conductive gel at every electrode, neoprene cap in size S, M, or L), streamed from NIC2 to iMotions Lab 11.1 through Lab Streaming Layer with the Enobio device profile. Electrode quality was checked in the NIC2 Liveview before recording, and the last status at F3 and F4 (Red, Yellow, or Green) was logged. [Record here the reference and ground placement and the final impedance of each channel; publication guidelines ask for both (Keil et al., 2014).] The NIC2 protocol was assumed to place the electrodes in the lab manual order (Ch1 Fz, Ch2 Cz, Ch3 Pz, Ch4 Oz, Ch5 F3, Ch6 F4, Ch7 P3, Ch8 P4). Sessions ran from 2027-02-01 to 2027-02-22, and setting up the cap, gel, and impedance took M = 16.8 minutes (SD = 3.3, range 10 to 25) for the respondents who were analyzed.

EEG was processed with the iMotions EEG Frontal Asymmetry R Notebook, with Ch5 (F3) as the left and Ch6 (F4) as the right frontal electrode set, the Alpha band (8 to 12 Hz), Welch power spectra (Welch, 1967) in 1 s windows with 50 percent overlap, a 0.5 to 100 Hz band-pass filter, a 60 Hz notch filter, and an artifact threshold of 120 microvolts, which removes samples at or above that amplitude and drops every window that contains one. In each window the notebook computes FA = ln(alpha power at F4) - ln(alpha power at F3). Because alpha power falls as cortical activity rises, a higher FA indicates relatively greater left frontal activity, which is interpreted as approach motivation (Allen et al., 2004; Smith et al., 2017). The EEG Frontal Asymmetry Metrics export supplied the mean FA of each stimulus and of two stimulus annotations, the KeyScene (32 to 38 s of Commercial_1A) and the EndScene (the last 10 s of each commercial, the window used in the iMotions demo study). The EEG Power Spectral Density R Notebook, run with the same filters on the Theta and Alpha bands, supplied frontal midline theta at Ch1. Because each respondent carries a stable asymmetry of their own, every score was baseline corrected by subtracting the FA of the baseline that immediately preceded the commercial. Before data collection we fixed an exclusion rule: a respondent was excluded when more than 25 percent of the F3 or F4 samples were removed in any baseline or commercial, or when the session log recorded a Red quality status at F3 or F4 that could not be fixed. Three respondents were excluded: P27 (Version A; F4 stayed Red after re-seating, added gel, and a lead swap, and 41.78 percent of the F4 samples were removed in Commercial_1A), P41 (Version A; an LSL stream gap of about 8.6 s in Commercial_2 followed by drift on all channels, with 30.42 percent of the F4 samples removed), and P45 (Version B; heavy movement and blinking, with 30.64 percent of the F3 samples removed in Commercial_1B). The analyzed sample comprised 45 respondents (Version A n = 22, Version B n = 23; 24 female, 21 male; age M = 21.7, SD = 2.4, range 18 to 26).

H1 was tested with a one sample t test on the KeyScene change score of the Version A respondents (equivalent to a paired t test of the KeyScene against its baseline), H2 with Welch's t test on the EndScene change of Commercial 1 between the two versions (Delacre et al., 2017), and H3 with a paired t test on the whole commercial change scores of Commercial_2 and Commercial 1. Effect sizes are Cohen's d_z for the within subjects tests and Cohen's d and Hedges' g for H2, each with a 95 percent confidence interval from the noncentral t distribution (Lakens, 2013). Each t test was checked with a Wilcoxon signed rank or Mann Whitney test, and normality was checked with Shapiro Wilk tests and QQ plots. For H2 we report the number of respondents per version that 80 percent power would require at the observed d. Because each respondent contributed two commercial change scores and saw the commercials in counterbalanced order, H3 was also tested with a linear mixed effects model with commercial, version, their interaction, and viewing position (first or second) as fixed effects, each coded -0.5 and +0.5, and a random intercept for respondent, fitted by REML with lme4 (Bates et al., 2015) and tested with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom (lmerTest); the intraclass correlation (ICC) was computed from the variance components. Exploratory analyses correlated each commercial's FA change with its liking rating (Pearson r with Fisher z intervals and Spearman rho) and described the change in frontal midline theta. Sensitivity analyses repeated H1 and H2 with a stricter 15 percent threshold and without baseline correction. All tests were two tailed with alpha = .05; H1 and H2 were directional, so they count as supported only when significant in the predicted direction, and no correction was applied across hypotheses. As a teaching contrast, H1 was also computed from an export made with the notebook's default Enobio channels (left Ch1, right Ch7).

## (b) Results

**Data quality and baselines.** In the 45 kept respondents the median percentage of samples removed per stimulus and channel was 1.46 (90th percentile 6.01, maximum 22.83). Nine kept respondents had a Red status at the first impedance check that was fixed before recording, and 19 of the 48 respondents were recorded with a Yellow status at F3 or F4, which the rule allows. The excluded respondent with the lost F4 contact would have been a clear outlier: this respondent's stimulus FA values ranged from 0.630 to 0.762, against -0.357 to 0.385 for all kept respondents. An FA computed from the PSD export, (Ch6 alpha minus Ch5 alpha in dB) divided by 4.343, agreed closely with the exported FA (r = .994 over 292 rows, mean difference 0.0008). Mean baseline alpha power was highest at the channels assumed to be Oz (12.63 dB) and Pz (11.77 dB) and lowest at the three frontal channels (8.12 to 8.25 dB), the pattern expected under the assumed montage. Baseline FA was M = -0.052 (SD = 0.130) for Baseline_1 and M = -0.054 (SD = 0.128) for Baseline_2, t(44) = -0.21, p = .834; the two baselines were strongly correlated (r = .82), and 61 of the 90 baseline values (67.8 percent) were negative.

**Key scene (H1).** Among the Version A respondents (n = 22), FA rose from M = -0.090 (SD = 0.104) in the baseline before Commercial_1A to M = 0.008 (SD = 0.157) during the key scene, a mean change of 0.097 (SD = 0.142), 95% CI [0.034, 0.160], t(21) = 3.21, p = .004, d_z = 0.68, 95% CI [0.21, 1.14]. The Wilcoxon signed rank test agreed (V = 210, p = .007), and the changes showed no departure from normality (Shapiro Wilk p = .821). Seventeen of the 22 respondents had a higher FA during the key scene than in their baseline. H1 was supported. Key scene scores were noisier than whole commercial scores: the SD of the change was 0.142 for the 6 s key scene against 0.098 for the whole Commercial_1A, and the key scene FA correlated less with its baseline (r = .46) than the whole commercial did (r = .63).

**End scene by version (H2).** The baseline corrected FA during the end scene of Commercial 1 was higher in Version A (M = 0.033, SD = 0.124, n = 22) than in Version B (M = -0.012, SD = 0.123, n = 23), a difference of 0.045, 95% CI [-0.029, 0.119], but the difference was not significant, Welch t(42.87) = 1.22, p = .229, Hedges' g = 0.36, 95% CI [-0.22, 0.94] (Cohen's d = 0.36, 95% CI [-0.23, 0.95]). The Mann Whitney test agreed (W = 313, p = .177). H2 was not supported. The interval is compatible both with no difference and with a large one, so the result is inconclusive rather than evidence of no effect. At the observed d = 0.36, a study would need 120 respondents per version (240 in total) for 80 percent power (two tailed, alpha = .05); with 22 and 23 respondents the power at this effect size was 0.22. By comparison, a within subjects test at the H1 effect size (d_z = 0.68) needs 19 respondents.

**Commercial_2 versus Commercial 1 (H3).** Across the 45 kept respondents, the whole commercial FA change was M = -0.029 (SD = 0.081) for Commercial 1 and M = 0.004 (SD = 0.085) for Commercial_2. The difference, 0.033 (SD = 0.112), 95% CI [-0.001, 0.066], fell just short of significance, t(44) = 1.98, p = .055, d_z = 0.29, 95% CI [-0.01, 0.59], whereas the Wilcoxon signed rank test fell just below .05 (V = 703, p = .037); 30 of the 45 respondents had a larger change for Commercial_2. The mixed model gave the same picture: the commercial effect was b = 0.0323, SE = 0.0167, t(42.0) = 1.94, p = .059, 95% CI [-0.0013, 0.0660]. Neither version (b = 0.0021, SE = 0.0186, t(43.0) = 0.11, p = .911) nor viewing position (second minus first, b = 0.0006, SE = 0.0167, t(42.0) = 0.04, p = .971) had an effect, and the commercial by version interaction was not significant, b = -0.0450, SE = 0.0334, t(42.0) = -1.35, p = .184, 95% CI [-0.1123, 0.0223]. The ICC was .110 after adjustment for the fixed effects (.069 without them), so little of the variance in the change scores lay between respondents. H3 was not supported at alpha = .05; the estimate points toward a slightly more positive response to Commercial_2, but the evidence is weak.

**Exploratory analyses.** Liking was M = 5.55 (SD = 1.26) for Commercial_1A, M = 4.96 (SD = 1.58) for Commercial_1B, and M = 5.31 (SD = 1.35) for Commercial_2. The FA change correlated weakly and not significantly with liking for Commercial 1, r(43) = .17, 95% CI [-.13, .44], p = .261 (Spearman rho = .23, p = .130), and moderately for Commercial_2, r(43) = .29, 95% CI [-.00, .54], p = .051 (rho = .29, p = .056). Nineteen of the 22 Version A respondents (86.4 percent) recalled the key scene, and 34 of 45 (75.6 percent) named the brand correctly. Frontal midline theta (Ch1, assumed Fz) was higher during both commercials than during the preceding baselines, by 0.44 dB (SD = 0.74, 95% CI [0.22, 0.66]) for Commercial 1 and 0.35 dB (SD = 0.59, 95% CI [0.17, 0.53]) for Commercial_2, with 33 and 31 of the 45 respondents showing a rise, while alpha power averaged over all eight channels fell by 0.51 dB during both commercials. These are descriptions; no hypothesis about theta was tested.

**Sensitivity analyses.** The stricter 15 percent threshold removed four more respondents (P04 and P18 in Version A, P22 and P32 in Version B, whose largest percentages removed ranged from 17.28 to 22.83), leaving 41 (20 and 21). H1 held, with a mean change of 0.100, 95% CI [0.031, 0.169], t(19) = 3.03, p = .007, d_z = 0.68, 95% CI [0.18, 1.16] (V = 174, p = .011), and H2 remained nonsignificant, with a difference of 0.040, 95% CI [-0.040, 0.119], Welch t(38.63) = 1.01, p = .318, g = 0.31, 95% CI [-0.30, 0.91] (W = 255, p = .246). Without baseline correction the key scene effect could not be seen: the raw KeyScene FA (M = 0.008, SD = 0.157) did not differ from zero, t(21) = 0.23, p = .819, d = 0.05, 95% CI [-0.37, 0.47]. The raw end scene comparison even reversed in sign, with a difference of -0.021, 95% CI [-0.105, 0.063], Welch t(40.36) = -0.50, p = .619, g = -0.15, 95% CI [-0.72, 0.43]: by chance the Version A baselines were 0.066 lower than the Version B baselines, and the pooled SD of the raw scores (0.141) was larger than that of the change scores (0.123). No conclusion of the planned analysis changed under the stricter threshold, and the uncorrected analysis shows why the planned tests use change scores.

**Teaching contrast: the default channels.** When FA was computed with the notebook's default Enobio channels (left Ch1 and right Ch7, which under the assumed montage are Fz and P3), the baseline "FA" averaged 0.681 (SD = 0.180) instead of -0.053, and the key scene effect disappeared: the mean change was -0.011 (SD = 0.123), 95% CI [-0.065, 0.044], t(21) = -0.40, p = .691, d_z = -0.09, 95% CI [-0.50, 0.33] (V = 118, p = .795), and these changes were nearly unrelated to the F3 and F4 changes (r = .17).

**Assumption checks.** Shapiro Wilk tests showed no departure from normality for the H1 changes (p = .821), the two H2 groups (Version A p = .962, Version B p = .061), the H3 differences (p = .558), the mixed model residuals (p = .675), or the respondent intercepts (p = .876), and the H2 group variances were nearly equal (ratio 1.02). (All values simulated.)

## (c) Discussion

In these simulated data, the key scene moved frontal asymmetry toward relatively greater left frontal activity than the baseline before the commercial, a medium to large within subjects effect (d_z = 0.68) that held under a stricter artifact rule. In the frontal asymmetry literature such a shift is read as approach motivation (Harmon-Jones & Gable, 2018), and the design follows the scene level comparison that Ohme and colleagues used to study a Sony Bravia commercial and its frog scene with EEG (Ohme, Matukin, & Szczurko, 2010; Ohme, Reykowska, et al., 2010). Whether the key scene carried over to the end of the commercial, where the brand appears, remains open. The end scene difference pointed the predicted way but was small and imprecise (g = 0.36, 95% CI [-0.22, 0.94]), and with 22 and 23 respondents the study had about one chance in five of detecting an effect of that size; a nonsignificant result of this kind says the study could not tell, not that the effect is absent. Commercial_2 drew a slightly more positive change than Commercial 1, but the evidence was weak: the t test (p = .055) and the Wilcoxon test (p = .037) fell on opposite sides of .05, which is a reminder to report the estimate and its interval rather than to sort results by a threshold. Frontal midline theta rose and alpha fell during both commercials, the pattern that accompanies attention and processing load, but with no hypothesis this stays a description.

Several limitations are typical of FA studies of commercials and would apply to a real version of this study. First, the data are simulated for teaching and were generated to follow the planned effects described in `make_simulated_data.py`; they demonstrate the analysis, not the effect. Second, the channel map was assumed. The Enobio reports Ch1 to Ch8, and only the NIC2 protocol says which scalp site each channel holds; the teaching contrast shows that the notebook's default channels (Ch1 and Ch7, Fz and P3 under the assumed montage) erase the key scene effect without any warning in the export, so the map, the reference, and the left and right electrode sets belong in every methods section (Keil et al., 2014). Third, the key scene lasts 6 s, which gives only 11 one second Welch windows against 119 for a whole commercial; key scene scores were noisier (SD of the change 0.142 against 0.098) and correlated less with the baseline, and asymmetry scores are dependable only when enough clean data enter them (see Towers & Allen, 2009, on estimating their reliability), so short scenes need more respondents, longer windows, or repeated exposures. Fourth, H2 compared different respondents. Between subjects designs leave the differences between people in the error term, and at the observed effect size a conclusive test would need about 120 respondents per version, more than five times the present sample. Fifth, each commercial was compared with its own preceding baseline, two different 60 s nature videos. The baselines were comparable here, but the second baseline follows a commercial and a rating slide, any drift over the session enters the change score, and 60 s is shorter than the baseline periods in the lab manual. Sixth, FA indexes approach and withdrawal motivation, not liking: approach can accompany anger or curiosity (Harmon-Jones & Gable, 2018), and the correlations between FA change and liking were weak to moderate (r = .17 and .29). Seventh, reading a mental state such as "the viewers liked the key scene" from a brain measure is reverse inference, which holds only to the extent that the measure is specific to that state (Plassmann et al., 2015); FA also depends on recording and processing choices such as the reference and the electrode pair (Allen et al., 2004; Smith et al., 2017). Eighth, the exclusion rule was written before collection and its reasons are logged, but the threshold is a choice: the 120 microvolt threshold catches large artifacts and leaves smaller blinks and muscle activity in the frontal channels, the manuals give both 120 and 100 microvolts, and the artifact report used here is a modeled table; the 15 percent sensitivity analysis shows that the conclusions did not depend on the 25 percent cut. Finally, the study used a single pair of commercials and one key scene, so the results describe these stimuli; claims about commercials or key scenes in general require several stimuli treated as a random factor (Judd et al., 2012).
