Reading list for the workshop on EEG with a Neuroelectrics Enobio 8 in iMotions, using frontal alpha asymmetry as an index of approach and withdrawal motivation in video advertising, with a worked example modeled on the Sony Bravia commercials Balls and Paint and the frog scene in Balls. Every entry below was checked on 2026-10-10 against the publisher, repository, library, or index pages that its verification line names, and the bibliographic details are recorded as those pages returned them; fields that no page showed are left blank rather than guessed. Entries are in APA 7 style and sorted alphabetically within each group.

The iMotions Sony Bravia demo article says the demo was inspired by two articles by Ohme and colleagues (2010), and its own reference list names them: Ohme, Reykowska, Wiener, and Choromanska (2010) in the Journal of Economic Psychology, which compared Sony Bravia commercials with frontal alpha asymmetry, and Ohme, Matukin, and Szczurko (2010) in der markt, which discusses the frog scene in Balls. The 2009 article by Ohme and colleagues in the Journal of Neuroscience, Psychology, and Economics is a separate study of a skin care advertisement.

Core list: 39 sources. Counts by group: Advertising and consumer neuroscience with EEG = 13; Frontal asymmetry theory and method = 8; EEG workload and synchrony = 3; EEG methods and reporting = 6; Statistics and reporting = 7; Ethics = 2.
