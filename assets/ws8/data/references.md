Reading list for the workshop on analyzing a multimodal biometric advertising study and reporting it twice, once as an APA results and discussion section and once as a client report: estimating effect sizes with confidence intervals rather than reporting bare p values, planning power and sample size, analyzing repeated measures with mixed models and correcting for multiple comparisons, separating planned from exploratory analyses under the APA reporting standards, building figures that show the data, interpreting eye tracking, facial expression, and skin conductance results without overclaiming, and communicating results and their uncertainty to clients and other nonspecialists. Every new entry below was checked on 2026-10-11 against the publisher, repository, library, or index pages that its verification line names, and the bibliographic details are recorded as those pages returned them; fields that no page showed are left blank rather than guessed. Entries reused from the Workshop 2, 3, 5, and 7 lists keep their earlier verification lines, which end with a note naming the list they come from. The Software group gives the citation for R as the installed R returns it and the citation for iMotions in the format the vendor publishes. Entries are in APA 7 style and sorted alphabetically within each group.

No retraction notice appeared for any entry. Two entries carry published corrections that students should read with the article: Button and colleagues (2013) has an Erratum that corrects the definition of R, the pre-study odds, on page 366, and Franconeri and colleagues (2021) has a Corrigendum (2022) that replaces Figures 5 and 23 and revises the text on Figure 20. The raincloud plot article (Allen and colleagues) is cited in version 2 (2021), the version whose line records two approved peer reviews.

Core list: 50 sources. Counts by group: Effect sizes, confidence intervals, and p values = 7; Power and sample size = 8; Repeated measures, mixed models, and multiple comparisons = 8; Planned and exploratory analyses and reporting standards = 6; Data visualization = 6; Multimodal and neuromarketing measurement and interpretation = 8; Communicating results to clients and nonspecialists = 5; Software = 2.
