Running a Glasses Study with Pupil Labs Neon in iMotions Lab 11.1

From the research question to exported AOI metrics, in the order the work actually happens. Written for the Martin's Family Clothing Consumer Behavior Research Laboratory and its Neon "Just act natural" frame, and checked against the iMotions Help Center and the lab's own iMotions correspondence.

  • About 90 minutes to read and tryEstimated time
  • Neon glasses, companion phone, lab laptop with iMotions Lab 11.1, a Wi-Fi network both can joinEquipment
  • Eye Tracking Glasses module and, for section 9, the Automated AOIs moduleLicenses used
  • Workshop 1 of the lab seriesSeries

Why this matters

Glasses studies are the lab's path into real places: a shelf at a local retailer, signage at a campus event, a batter's view from the plate. The recording is the easy part. The time goes into mapping gaze onto reference images and defining areas of interest, and a study designed without that in mind produces hours of video that cannot be compared across participants. This workshop puts the analysis decisions at the front, where they belong.

What the Neon is, in one paragraph

Neon is a wearable eye tracker from Pupil Labs that estimates gaze with a neural network rather than a per-person calibration. It records binocular gaze at 200 Hz and a forward-facing scene video at 30 Hz, plus head motion from an inertial sensor, and it stores the recording on an Android companion phone. iMotions lists the uncalibrated accuracy at about 1.8 degrees. A charged unit records for roughly four hours. The lab's frame is the standard adult "Just act natural" frame; the under-helmet "Ready set go" frame is the one iMotions recommended for batters.

Glasses or the Aurora? A quick decision
  • The stimulus is a screen, an image, a video, or a website: use the Aurora. Analysis is faster because the stimulus is identical for everyone.
  • The participant moves, handles objects, or looks at a physical space: use the Neon.
  • The question is about fine timing (saccades, microsaccades): neither is the right tool at the lab's current setup; design around fixation and dwell measures.
1

Plan the study around the analysis

Every decision here saves hours later. Gaze mapping needs a reference image per scene; AOIs need a master list; comparisons need enough usable recordings.

  1. Write the question as a comparison. For example: which of two shelf arrangements draws earlier and longer attention to the client's product? A comparison tells you the conditions, the AOIs, and the metrics in one sentence.
    starting with "let us see where people look" and deciding the comparison after collection.
  2. Choose the task. Find a product, choose a product, browse for a fixed time, or complete a purchase. Tasks with a clear start and end make time windows easy to define during gaze mapping.
  3. Decide the design. Between-subjects when the environment cannot be reset between conditions; within-subjects with counterbalanced order when it can. Record condition and order as respondent variables.
  4. Set a sample target with losses built in. A working heuristic for field eye tracking is 20 to 30 usable recordings per condition; plan to recruit about 25 percent more to cover recordings lost to fit, lighting, or mapping failures. Justify with a power estimate if the study is for publication.
  5. List the scenes that need a reference image. One image per fixed view participants will spend time on: the shelf, the end cap, the sign. Plan to photograph each one from a typical viewing position before the first participant arrives.
  6. Write the AOI master list. Name every area you will measure (Brand_A_Logo, Price_Tag_Row2, Promo_Sign) and keep the names identical across conditions. This list is pasted into the AOI window later.
  7. Handle permissions. Venue permission in writing, IRB consent that names the scene video and gaze recording, and signage or a script for bystanders who may appear in the scene camera.
  8. Decide whether other sensors join. GSR during a choice task is possible, but walking adds motion artifacts. If yes, the study type in section 2 changes.

Recommendation for the lab's first client shelf study

Use Import glasses data. Campus and store Wi-Fi networks usually block device-to-device traffic, so the live connection in section 3 often fails outside the lab. Starting and stopping on the phone costs nothing, and the import path is identical. Move to live recording once you have run it successfully in the lab with your own router.

3

Connect the Neon to iMotions (live recording only)

Skip this section for Import or Sensor Sync studies. For live recording, the laptop talks to the companion phone over Wi-Fi.

  1. Check the versions. iMotions 10.1.13 or newer supports the current companion app line; the lab's 11.1 build qualifies. The phone shipped with the glasses runs a supported Android version; do not upgrade Android on the companion phone without checking Pupil Labs' compatibility list.
    Pupil Labs documentation: compatible devices
  2. Plug the glasses into the phone with the USB-C cable that came in the case. Other cables may power the unit without carrying data.
  3. Open the Neon Companion app and create a wearer the first time. A Pupil Cloud log-in is required by the standard app.
  4. Join the same Wi-Fi network on both devices. Confirm in Windows settings on the laptop and Android settings on the phone.
    institutional or public networks that block device-to-device traffic. If the connection test fails on campus, a small travel router with its own network is the reliable fix.
  5. In iMotions, open Global Settings from the Preferences menu, go to the Eye tracking tab, enable Eye Tracking Server, and choose Neon Glasses from the list.
  6. Click Browse, pick the Neon Companion entry (it shows IP address, device name, serial number, and phone model), click Select, then Test Connection, then OK twice.
    a successful connection message. If the device does not appear, read the phone's IP address from Settings, About phone, Status, and type it into the Host Address box, then test again.
  7. If the app misbehaves, reboot the whole phone, not just the app, and reconnect. iMotions cannot see error states inside the companion app.
4

Build the study in the Study Builder

  1. Create a new study (New Study button or Ctrl+N) and name it with the lab convention, for example 2026_ClientName_Shelf.
  2. Select Glasses as the overall study type, then the glasses study type you chose in section 2 (Glasses live recording, or Import glasses data), then Neon Glasses as the eye tracking sensor.
  3. Add respondent variables for condition, order, and anything you will segment on later (store visited, group).
  4. Add the respondents you expect, or add them on the day using the lab ID format (last four digits of student ID plus first two letters of last name, or the client study's own format).
  5. For live recording: select a respondent and click Start Recording. The companion app shows recording status. Click End Recording in iMotions to stop the phone.
    the recording listed under the respondent, waiting for the file import in section 6.
  6. Zip a backup of the study before the field day, named with the lab's StudyName_backup_vN pattern.
5

Run the field day

Most lost recordings are lost here, to fit, light, and forgotten reference photos.

  1. Charge everything the night before: the phone, the laptop, and a power bank. Clear phone storage so there is room for the day's recordings.
  2. Photograph every reference scene from a typical viewing distance and height before the first participant, with the same lighting you expect during sessions. Name the images to match the AOI master list.
  3. Fit the glasses and run a short gaze check: ask the participant to look at three objects at different distances while you watch the live gaze overlay on the phone. If the dot sits consistently off, re-seat the frame and repeat. Note any remaining offset in the session log.
  4. Read the task instructions from a script so every participant hears the same words. Start the recording, then say a clear spoken marker ("Task begins now") so the start is visible in the audio and scene video.
  5. Stay out of the scene camera and keep bystanders aware of the recording per your IRB plan.
  6. Stop the recording with a second spoken marker, then run the post-task questions (choice made, recall, liking).
  7. Log each session: respondent ID, condition, start and end times, fit notes, lighting, interruptions. The log is what makes exclusions defensible later.
  8. Keep the environment consistent across participants: same shelf stock, same signage, low-traffic periods when the venue allows.
6

Transfer and import the recordings

The files move from the phone to a Data folder on the laptop, then into iMotions. The folder structure is strict.

  1. In the companion app, open the Recordings list (folder icon at the bottom), open the menu for each recording (the three dots), and choose Export.
  2. Connect the phone to the laptop with the supplied USB-C cable and open it in File Explorer: Internal shared storage, Documents, Neon Export.
  3. Build the Data folder: one parent folder for the study, one subfolder per respondent, each subfolder holding that recording's files. The import window expects, per recording: gaze ps1.raw, gaze ps1.time, Neon Scene Camera v1 ps1.time, Neon Scene Camera v1 ps1.mp4, extimu ps1.raw, extimu ps1.time, and info.json.
    dropping all recordings into one folder, or renaming files.
  4. Live recording study: select the respondent, and in the Recorded Data panel click Import, then browse to that respondent's folder.
    the sampling rate listed in the Recorded Data panel, and the scene video with gaze overlay in the Replay window.
  5. Import glasses data study: in the Study Builder choose Glasses, Import glasses data, Neon Glasses, then New study or Merge into an existing compatible study, click Next, Browse to the Data folder, and import. To align with a Sensor Sync study afterwards, right-click the imported study and choose Merge with another study.
  6. Optional: remove the fisheye distortion of the Neon scene camera during import if you want straight shelf edges for mapping and AOIs. iMotions offers this as an import option.
  7. If you need blink, pupil, or eye-state signals, enable Compute eye state and Compute fixations in the companion app (or in Pupil Cloud processing) before export; otherwise those columns are empty. Gaze and video import without Pupil Cloud.
Pupil Cloud as the alternative transfer route

With Cloud upload enabled in the app's settings, each recording can be uploaded from the Recordings menu, processed (roughly as long as the recording itself), and downloaded from the Pupil Cloud project as files that import the same way. Use it when the phone is not with you, or when you want the Cloud-only processed signals.

7

Create the analysis

  1. Hover over the study name in the Library and click Add Analysis. Name it and add comments (for example, which exclusion rules apply).
  2. Set segmentation criteria to include only usable respondents, by hand or by group variable. Pilot recordings stay out.
  3. Set up signal processing only if other sensors were recorded (for GSR with two Shimmers: hardware, then Peak detection, then a Peaks binned aggregation row with "treat each hardware as individual respondents" checked). For glasses-only studies, click Next.
  4. Select the stimuli (the glasses recordings) and click Add.
    the analysis in the drop-down under the study, and analysis buttons along the bottom of the window.
  5. Replay two recordings end to end before doing anything else. Confirm gaze overlays, audio markers, and task boundaries are where the log says they are.
8

Map gaze onto a reference image

Gaze mapping converts each participant's moving-scene gaze into coordinates on one still image, so heat maps, scan paths, and static AOIs can be computed across people. It is the longest step; budget time for it.

  1. Open the analysis, select a glasses recording in the stimulus menu, and click Gaze Map in the lower menu. Gaze mapping only appears for dynamic stimuli.
  2. Place the timeline cursor at the start of the task window, click the Gaze Mapping button, and choose a reference image source: From image (your photographed shelf) or From current frame (a clean frame of the video).
  3. Name the gaze map image and Save. The image now spans the timeline from the cursor to the end.
  4. Move the cursor to the end of the task window, right-click in the timeline band belonging to the gaze map image, and choose Start/end interval at the timeline position. Annotation intervals can be used instead if you created them.
  5. Right-click again and choose Queue gaze mapping processing, then Use full-image algorithm (the vertical-motion option is for scrolling screens, not shelves).
    the interval turning green where mapping succeeded. Blue stretches mean mapping did not succeed there, which can simply mean the participant looked away from the reference scene.
  6. Inspect the result by replaying: original gaze on the right, mapped gaze on the reference image on the left. Apply corrections where the mapped point drifts.
  7. Repeat for every other respondent using From existing, which maps onto the same image and aggregates everyone onto it.
  8. Speed up large studies: use Batch import images from a folder to load reference images for all respondents at once, and queue the mapping jobs as a batch on the laptop. Cloud batch processing exists but needs a separate license the lab does not hold.
How many reference images?

One per scene participants dwell on. A shelf study with a single shelf needs one image; a store walk with three displays needs three, each with its own time interval per respondent. Keep images the same for everyone in a condition, and keep the AOI master list aligned with the image names.

9

Draw or automate the areas of interest

On a gaze map, static AOIs are enough. On the raw moving video, you need dynamic AOIs, and the Automated AOIs module does most of that work.

Static AOIs on the gaze map

  1. Select the gaze map, click AOI, then Open analysis. The AOI window has a Stimulus panel for drawing, an AOI Timeline, an AOI Metrics panel, and an AOI Editor for exact coordinates.
  2. Click Draw AOI, choose a tool (rectangle, circle, polygon, or grid), pick a color, and draw each area from the master list. Name them exactly as listed. Assign a keyboard key to speed up drawing.
  3. Group AOIs where it helps (all competitor facings, all price tags) so you can report at both levels.
  4. Save an AOI template from the AOI menu so the second condition and future studies reuse the same shapes and names.

Dynamic AOIs on the moving video (Automated AOIs module)

  1. Check the machine first. Automated AOIs run best on a PC with an NVIDIA GPU; the first click on Automated AOI prompts an installation of the package. Do this in the lab, not on the client's day.
  2. Find an input frame where the object is clearly visible, then right-click the object and choose Automate, or open a new Input Frame from the panel.
  3. Choose a geometry type (rectangle, ellipse, or polygon) and mark the object: left-click adds positive points, right-click adds negative points where the mask spills outside the object, and Ctrl plus left-drag defines a region.
    an orange mask covering the object with a preview rectangle around it.
  4. Set Skip frames. The default of 2 is right for most shelf footage; lower it when the object moves constantly, raise it for slow scenes to save processing time.
  5. Process the video, then scrub the AOI Timeline: black dots mark input frames, key frames show where the shape was updated, and the AOI is interpolated between them. Add a second input frame where the object changes appearance or leaves and re-enters the view.
10

Pick the metrics and export

iMotions reports 48 AOI metrics in four groups. A client report needs five of them, chosen before you open the export.

QuestionMetric to reportGroup in the AOI window
Did people notice it at all?Respondent ratio (%)Fixation-based
How soon was it noticed?Time to first fixation; use the max variant to count non-fixators at the full exposure timeFixation-based
How much attention did it hold?Dwell time (ms) and Dwell time (%)Fixation-based
Did people come back to it?Revisit countFixation-based
Is the data trustworthy?Valid data (%) and Respondent dataInformation
  1. In the AOI window, use the drop-down lists on the left to show the metric group, and hover over the question-mark icons for the exact definitions. Gaze-based metrics use raw samples; fixation-based metrics apply the fixation classifier and are the ones to report.
  2. Record the fixation classifier settings used in the analysis (the I-VT filter is the common choice) so the methods section can state them.
  3. Export the AOI metrics to a dated Exports subfolder, and save a note beside the file listing respondents included, AOI template version, and classifier settings.
  4. Export a heat map and a scan path per condition from the gaze map for the client report; keep the statistics in the appendix.

Practice task: a two-person shelf pilot

Before any client work, run this in the lab with one colleague as the participant and one as the researcher, then swap.

  1. Set up a mock shelf with at least six distinct products on two rows. Photograph it from 1.2 meters.
  2. Build an Import glasses data study named 2026_Pilot_Shelf with two respondents and a condition variable.
  3. Record a 60-second "find the cheapest item" task for each person, starting and stopping in the companion app with spoken markers.
  4. Transfer, import, replay, create the analysis, and gaze map both recordings onto the shelf photo.
  5. Draw six product AOIs plus one price-row AOI, save the template, and export fixation-based metrics.
  6. Write three sentences a client could read: who looked first at what, which product held attention, and one caution about data quality. Time the whole exercise and note where the hours went.

Self-check quiz

1. The Neon recording is finished on the phone. What must happen before iMotions can analyze it, regardless of study type?

2. A gaze mapping interval shows a blue stretch in the middle. What does it mean?

3. You have mapped the first respondent. Which option maps the next respondent onto the same image and aggregates them?

4. On a gaze map, which kind of AOI is normally sufficient?

5. In the Automated AOI input frame, what does a right-click do?

6. Which metric answers "how soon was the product noticed" while still counting people who never fixated it?
